02 / OUR PROPOSED EXTENSION

Evaluate the intent, not just the word.

01

Separate two tasks

Pair a factual question with a question asking what a supplied statement claims. Keep the statement and model fixed.

02

Keep the controls

Use true and false statements, plus a factual baseline with no context. That gives five conditions for each example.

03

Score meaning separately

Have two reviewers label task-following and factual endorsement separately. Human review is the next step; it has not been completed.

This is a student evaluation extension inspired by the paper. It is not a claim of a new editing algorithm or an established research novelty. The planned 12-example, 60-output study has not been run.

03 / CONNECTION TO THE PAPER

Can Editing LLMs Inject Harm?

The paper investigates whether model editing can introduce harmful behavior. Its experiments include ROME, fine-tuning, and in-context editing (ICE). Our small demo explores the context-based part and how question wording affects interpretation.

We change prompt context, not model weights. Three simple examples on a 0.5B model do not reproduce the paper’s full experiments or establish safety performance.

What the evidence supports

  • Actual saved responses from one small local model.
  • Some responses report a claim while retaining factual answers.
  • Failures remain visible: the water baseline is wrong; the France attribution task is not followed under false context.
  • No validated harm rate, human-reviewed score, or defense improvement is claimed.
